SAP Learning Guide: From ERP Basics to Interview-Ready

A self-study reference built around your “SAP: An Introduction” slides (ERP, SAP history, landscapes, products, project types, roles, master and transactional data, modules, and SAP MM), extended into FICO, SD, PP/QM, ABAP, Basis, Fiori, S/4HANA and BTP.

Every topic has a summary, key concepts, verified links, YouTube search links, a hands-on exercise and interview questions. Every practice system listed is a legitimate, SAP-sanctioned route.

1. Introduction and how to use this guide

SAP is the largest ERP software provider. Its software runs finance, procurement, sales, production and HR for a large share of the world’s big companies, which is why SAP skills remain in demand. Your slides cover the vocabulary every beginner needs. This guide takes each slide topic and adds what the slides leave out: where to practise, what to build, and how it is tested in interviews.

What each topic section contains

  • Summary and key concepts, written for a beginner.
  • Resources, tagged Free, Trial or Paid.
  • YouTube: search links plus selection tips (see the note below).
  • Hands-on exercise, with the system you need.
  • Interview questions for that topic. The full bank with answers is in section 20.

Instead, each topic has a YouTube search link that works today, and the official SAP channel is linked below. When choosing a video, prefer: (1) SAP’s own channels, (2) uploads from the last two to three years, since S/4HANA changed many screens, (3) videos that show a real system rather than slides only.

4. ERP fundamentals

Summary

ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) is commercial software that integrates the processes needed to run a business, such as finance, HR, procurement, sales and production, on a shared database. Before ERP, each department kept its own records. A sales clerk could promise stock that purchasing had already consumed, and finance learned about it at month-end. In an ERP, one transaction updates every affected area at once.

Your slide’s ATM example is a good one: when you withdraw cash, the balance check and the debit happen against one live record, in real time, not in a nightly batch. ERP aims for the same behaviour across a company, which improves resource utilization and reduces waste (stock sitting idle, duplicated data entry, reconciliation work).

Key concepts

  • Integration: one transaction, many consequences. A goods receipt updates inventory, the accounting ledger and the purchase order status.
  • Single source of truth: one customer record, one material record.
  • Real-time processing versus batch processing.
  • Standard software versus custom code: standard processes are configured, not programmed.
  • Business process areas: Procure-to-Pay, Order-to-Cash, Plan-to-Produce, Record-to-Report, Hire-to-Retire.

Resources

  • Free SAP Learning: Learning Journeys. Start with the introductory S/4HANA and “business process” journeys; use the Level and Product filters.
  • Free openSAP. SAP’s MOOC platform. Its home page now states it has moved to the SAP Learning site, so search courses on learning.sap.com if a link redirects.
  • Free Alison: An Introduction to SAP S/4HANA. Third-party beginner course. The course is free; a certificate may cost extra.
  • YouTube “What is ERP” search and “SAP business processes overview” search.

Hands-on exercise: ERP before and after

  1. Choose a small business (a bakery, a clinic, a bike shop).
  2. List the five spreadsheets or tools it would use without ERP.
  3. Draw one sale: what must change in inventory, cash, and tax records?
  4. Mark every place data would be re-typed or could go out of sync.

Deliverable: a one-page diagram (draw.io) with at least five integration points.

5. SAP: history, versions, architecture

Summary

SAP was founded in 1972 by five former IBM engineers and is headquartered in Walldorf, Germany. Its software evolved with computing itself: from mainframe systems to client/server to in-memory databases to the cloud. Knowing this evolution lets you place any system you meet at work (many companies still run several generations at once).

Version timeline

GenerationEraWhat to remember
R/11970sFirst real-time accounting system. Taught as a single-tier design: presentation, application and database together.
R/2Late 1970sMainframe version. Taught as two-tier: presentation separate, application and database together.
R/31992Client/server. Three tiers: presentation, application, database on separate systems. This design dominated for decades.
ECC (ERP Central Component)2000sThe mature R/3 successor, widely installed. ECC 6.0 is the usual reference. SAP has announced end dates for standard maintenance, so check SAP’s current statement for the exact year.
S/4HANA2015 onwardBuilt for the SAP HANA in-memory database. Simplified data model, Fiori user interface. Available as on-premise, private cloud and public cloud editions.

Three-tier architecture

  • Presentation layer: what the user sees (SAP GUI, browser with Fiori).
  • Application layer: the application servers that run business logic and ABAP programs.
  • Database layer: where all data is stored (SAP HANA in modern systems).

Separating the tiers lets a company add application servers to handle more users without touching the database, and lets the database be tuned independently.

Resources

  • Free SAP Help Portal. Official product documentation; search “SAP S/4HANA” then “architecture”.
  • Free SAP Learning: look for ABAP Platform and S/4HANA fundamentals journeys.
  • Free SAP News Center for company history and announcements.
  • YouTube “SAP three-tier architecture” search and “ECC vs S/4HANA” search.

Hands-on exercise: three diagrams and a table

  1. Draw R/1, R/2 and R/3 as boxes showing which layers share a server.
  2. For each, write one advantage and one limitation (scaling, cost, maintenance).
  3. Build a table comparing ECC and S/4HANA: database, user interface, data model, deployment options.

Deliverable: a two-page PDF you could use to explain this to a colleague.

Interview questions

Explain three-tier architecture. Why did SAP move from R/2 to R/3? What are the main differences between ECC and S/4HANA?

6. System landscape and transports

Summary

As your slides say, SAP is divided into three landscapes: Development (where all configuration and customizing is done), Quality (testing and user training) and Production (the live server). Changes are made once in Development and then moved forward, so that nothing untested ever reaches live business data.

Key concepts

  • Client: a self-contained business unit inside a system, with its own master data and users. A system can host several clients.
  • Transport request: the package that carries a change from one system to the next. Workbench requests carry development objects; Customizing requests carry configuration.
  • Transport route: DEV → QAS → PRD, controlled by the Change and Transport System.
  • Sandbox: an optional system for experiments that should never be transported.
  • Cutover: the planned move to production at go-live.
TransactionPurpose
SE09 / SE10Transport Organizer: view and manage your requests.
STMSTransport Management System: import queues and routes.
SCC4Client settings.
SPROCustomizing: the configuration tree (the “IMG”).

Transaction codes can differ between ECC and S/4HANA, and many tasks now have a Fiori app as well. Confirm a code in your own release before relying on it.

Resources

  • Free SAP Help Portal: search “Change and Transport System”.
  • Free SAP Learning: the ABAP Platform fundamentals material includes the application lifecycle and transport overview.
  • YouTube “SAP landscape and transports” search.

Hands-on exercise: follow a change through the landscape

  1. Scenario: finance asks for a new payment term “net 45 days”.
  2. Write the path: who requests, who configures in DEV, which request type, what testing happens in QAS, who approves import to PRD.
  3. If you have a trial system, open SPRO and locate where payment terms are maintained, then note which request type it would create.

Deliverable: a swim-lane diagram with testing checkpoints and sign-offs.

Interview questions

Why three systems and not one? What is a transport request? What happens if someone configures directly in production?

9. Data in SAP and navigation

Summary

Master data is the long-lived reference data that business activity is built on: materials, vendors, customers, bank details, cost centres. Transactional data is what users create when they do business with that master data: purchase orders, goods receipts, invoices, sales orders. Master data changes rarely; transactional data is created constantly. Errors in master data multiply into every transaction that uses it, which is why data governance is a real project workstream.

Master dataTransactional data
ExamplesMaterial, vendor / business partner, customer, GL accountPurchase order, goods receipt, invoice, sales order, delivery
Change frequencyLowHigh
Created byData owners, master data teamEnd users in daily work
Risk if wrongErrors repeat in every transactionSingle-document errors, correctable by reversal

Navigation basics

  • SAP Easy Access menu and the command field, where you type a transaction code (add /n to switch, /o to open a new session).
  • Fiori launchpad: tile-based browser interface used in S/4HANA.
  • Display tools: SE16N (table display, restricted in many systems), SE11 (data dictionary).
  • User defaults and parameters: SU3.

In S/4HANA, vendors and customers are maintained as Business Partners (transaction BP), replacing the old separate vendor and customer master transactions. The old codes may still appear in documentation and older training videos.

Resources

  • Free SAP Learning: navigation and “getting started with SAP S/4HANA” content (search the catalogue for “navigation”).
  • Free SAP Help Portal: search “Business Partner” and “material master”.
  • YouTube “master vs transactional data” search, “SAP navigation basics” search.

Hands-on exercise: master data tour (needs a trial system)

  1. Log in. Open the display transaction for a material (MM03 or the Fiori app for product master data) and walk the views: Basic data, Purchasing, MRP, Accounting.
  2. Display a business partner with a vendor role. Note company code data vs purchasing data.
  3. Pick one field (for example, valuation class or payment terms) and write: if this were wrong, which downstream transactions would be affected?

Deliverable: a one-page “master data cheat sheet” with screenshots.

10. SAP MM in depth

Summary

SAP MM (Materials Management) is part of SAP’s logistics functions. It handles procurement, inventory management and the valuation of materials, and it is the backbone that connects to SD (sales), PP (production) and FI (finance). It covers planning to control: deciding what to buy and when, buying it, receiving and storing it, and paying for it, with every step recorded in finance automatically. Your slides list its features: procurement process, master data, account determination and material valuation, inventory management, invoice verification and MRP.

Enterprise structure (organizational levels)

LevelMeaning
ClientHighest level; a self-contained unit in the system.
Company codeIndependent legal entity with its own books.
PlantLocation where materials are produced, procured or stored.
Storage locationPlace within a plant where stock is kept.
Purchasing organizationNegotiates conditions with vendors.
Purchasing groupThe buyer or buyer team.

Master data

  • Material master: organized in views (basic data, purchasing, MRP, storage, accounting). The material type (for example raw material, finished product, trading goods, service) controls which views exist and how stock is valued.
  • Vendor / business partner: general, company code and purchasing data.
  • Purchasing info record: links a vendor to a material with price and delivery terms.
  • Source list and quota arrangement: control which vendors may supply a material and in what proportion.

The procurement cycle

  1. Requirement determination: manually or from MRP.
  2. Purchase requisition (PR): an internal request to buy.
  3. Source determination and RFQ: choose a vendor; request for quotation and quotation comparison where needed.
  4. Purchase order (PO): the binding order to the vendor.
  5. PO follow-up: confirmations, reminders.
  6. Goods receipt (GR): stock increases and the accounting entry is posted.
  7. Invoice verification: the vendor’s invoice is checked against PO and GR (the “three-way match”).
  8. Payment: handled in finance (FI).

Inventory management and movement types

Every stock change is a goods movement identified by a three-digit movement type. Each movement creates a material document (what moved) and, if value changes, an accounting document (what it cost). Common examples:

Movement typeMeaning
101Goods receipt for a purchase order (102 reverses it).
122Return to vendor.
201Goods issue to a cost centre.
261Goods issue to a production order.
301Plant-to-plant transfer.
311Transfer between storage locations in a plant.
561Initial stock entry (data migration).
601Goods issue for delivery (from SD).

Material valuation and account determination

  • Price control: Standard price (S) values stock at a fixed price, with differences posted to a price-difference account. Moving average price (V) recalculates the price with each receipt.
  • Valuation class: a field on the material that, together with the movement type, decides which general-ledger accounts are posted.
  • Automatic account determination: configured in the transaction OBYC. Key transaction keys to know: BSX (stock account), WRX (goods receipt / invoice receipt clearing, the “GR/IR” account), GBB (offsetting entries for goods issues and adjustments) and PRD (price differences).

The classic example: with a PO for 100 units at 10 each, the goods receipt debits stock (BSX) 1,000 and credits GR/IR (WRX) 1,000. When the invoice arrives, it debits GR/IR 1,000 and credits the vendor 1,000. The GR/IR account clears to zero when everything matches.

Invoice verification

The invoice is posted against the PO (Fiori app or MIRO). The system checks quantity and price tolerances. A mismatch blocks the invoice for review. Typical issues: invoice before goods receipt, price differences, partial deliveries, tax codes.

MRP (Material Requirements Planning)

MRP compares requirements (sales orders, forecasts, production needs) with available stock and open supply, then proposes planned orders or purchase requisitions. Key concepts: MRP type (for example MRP vs reorder-point planning), lot-sizing procedure, safety stock, planned delivery time, MRP controller, and the stock/requirements list (MD04). In S/4HANA, MRP runs are faster and a Fiori app set replaces much of the classic MRP transaction family.

Hands-on exercise A: complete procure-to-pay

System: S/4HANA trial appliance or the SAP Best Practices test scripts.

  1. Display the material master of a purchased raw material. Record material type, valuation class and price control.
  2. Create a purchase requisition, convert it to a purchase order, and note the document numbers.
  3. Post the goods receipt (movement 101). Open the material document and the accounting document. Write down the debit and credit accounts.
  4. Post the vendor invoice against the PO. Confirm the GR/IR account clears.
  5. Check stock (MMBE) and the PO history.

Deliverable: a document with screenshots at each step and a short paragraph per posting explaining why those accounts were hit.

Hands-on exercise B: break it on purpose

  1. Post a goods receipt for 90 units against a PO for 100. Observe the PO status.
  2. Post an invoice for the full 100. What does the system do? Why?
  3. Reverse the goods receipt (movement 102). What changes in stock and finance?
  4. Run MRP for a material with a reorder level. Explain each planned order or requisition it proposes.

Deliverable: a one-page error log: what happened, why, how you fixed it. This is exactly what support consultants do.

11. SAP FICO

Summary

FICO combines FI (Financial Accounting, which produces the legal books for external reporting) and CO (Controlling, which analyses costs and profitability for internal management). Every other module posts into finance, so FICO knowledge helps in any SAP career.

Key concepts

  • Organizational units: company code, chart of accounts, controlling area, fiscal year variant, ledger.
  • FI components: General Ledger (GL), Accounts Payable (AP, vendors), Accounts Receivable (AR, customers), Asset Accounting (AA), bank accounting.
  • CO components: cost centre accounting, internal orders, profit centre accounting, product costing, profitability analysis.
  • S/4HANA Universal Journal: one line-item table for FI and CO postings, which removes the old reconciliation between the two.
  • Period-end: accruals, depreciation run, foreign-currency valuation, closing periods.

12. SAP SD

Summary

SD (Sales and Distribution) handles the order-to-cash cycle: taking a customer order, checking availability, delivering, invoicing and receiving payment.

Key concepts

  • Organizational units: sales organization, distribution channel, division, sales area, shipping point.
  • Document flow: inquiry → quotation → sales order → delivery → goods issue → billing → payment. Each document references the previous one.
  • Pricing (condition technique): prices, discounts, surcharges and taxes are conditions determined through a pricing procedure.
  • Availability check (ATP): confirms whether the order can be delivered on the requested date.
  • Output and partner determination: which documents are printed or sent, and which business partners (sold-to, ship-to, bill-to) apply.
  • Integration: goods issue (movement 601) reduces stock in MM and posts cost of goods sold to FI; billing posts revenue and the customer receivable.

13. SAP PP and QM

Summary

PP (Production Planning) plans and executes manufacturing: what to make, with which materials, on which machines, when. QM (Quality Management) controls quality: inspecting incoming goods, in-process output and finished goods.

Key concepts

  • Bill of materials (BOM): the components needed to make a product.
  • Routing and work centre: the operations and the machines or teams that perform them.
  • Production order: the instruction to make a quantity of a product; involves goods issue of components (261) and goods receipt of the product.
  • MRP: drives planned orders for both purchased and manufactured items.
  • QM: inspection plan, inspection lot, results recording, usage decision, quality notification.

14. SAP ABAP

Summary

ABAP (Advanced Business Application Programming) is SAP’s programming language. Functional consultants configure; ABAP developers build what configuration cannot: reports, interfaces, forms, enhancements and, increasingly, cloud-ready extensions.

Key concepts

  • ABAP Dictionary: tables, data elements, domains, views.
  • Reports and ALV grids: classic report programming.
  • Object-oriented ABAP: classes, interfaces, exceptions.
  • Core Data Services (CDS): data models defined on the database.
  • ABAP RESTful Application Programming Model (RAP): the modern model for building Fiori apps and services.
  • Enhancements: BAdIs, user exits, enhancement spots. Modern guidance favours the “clean core” principle: extend without modifying SAP standard code.
  • ABAP Cloud: the restricted, upgrade-safe ABAP development model for S/4HANA Cloud and BTP.

Resources

  • Free ABAP Cloud Developer Trial (Docker), SAP Learning ABAP journeys.

15. SAP Basis

Summary

Basis is SAP’s technical administration: installing, running, monitoring and securing the system landscape. Basis administrators keep the system up, move transports, manage users and authorizations, and tune performance.

Key concepts

  • Users and authorizations: roles are built in PFCG; users are created in SU01.
  • Transports: STMS (see section 6).
  • Monitoring: work processes (SM50), system log (SM21), short dumps (ST22), background jobs (SM36 / SM37).
  • Connectivity: RFC destinations (SM59).
  • Database and kernel: HANA administration, patches and upgrades.
  • Lifecycle tools: SAP Solution Manager and SAP Cloud ALM.

16. Fiori, S/4HANA and BTP

Summary

SAP Fiori is the browser-based user experience used in S/4HANA, built around role-based tiles on a launchpad. S/4HANA brings a simplified data model, in-memory processing on SAP HANA and embedded analytics. SAP BTP is the platform for extensions, integration, data and AI, designed to keep the ERP core clean.

What changed for MM and the other modules

  • Vendor and customer master maintained as Business Partner.
  • Material documents in a single table; stock data simplified.
  • Universal Journal for finance.
  • Fiori apps for most daily tasks; many classic transactions still available for experts.
  • Material numbers can be longer than before.

19. Certification and career

Certification

SAP certifications are proctored exams that validate role-based knowledge. For SAP MM, the usual choice is the SAP Certified Associate: SAP S/4HANA Sourcing and Procurement, exam code C_TS452 (the code has a release suffix such as 2022 or 2410, so confirm the current version when you register). SAP says its certificates must be kept current through periodic assessments.

  • Free SAP Learning: each certification page links the recommended learning journey, and the site handles exam scheduling.
  • Paid The exam itself, and the SAP PRESS certification guide linked in section 10.
  • Free Students: learning.sap.com/students mentions a student edition of Learning Hub with live sessions and exam attempts. Check the page for current terms.

Do not use “brain dumps” or leaked exam questions. They breach SAP’s certification agreement and can get a certificate revoked. Use official learning journeys, a real system, and honest practice tests.

Career steps

  1. Pick a specialization and build one capstone.
  2. Publish your work and write short blog posts on SAP Community, which also builds a visible profile.
  3. Read real job descriptions and match your skills against them.
  4. Look for support, key-user or junior consultant roles. Many people enter through support projects.
  5. Add the certification, then keep learning: a second module or BTP.

20. Interview question bank

Open each question, answer it aloud first, then compare. Answers are intentionally short; expand them with examples from your own practice work.

Concepts and architecture

What is ERP, and why do companies use it?

ERP integrates core business processes (finance, procurement, sales, production, HR) on one shared database. Companies use it for a single source of truth, real-time information, less duplicate data entry and better control of resources.

What does SAP stand for, and what is SAP?

Systems, Applications and Products in Data Processing. SAP is a German software company and the largest ERP vendor; the name also refers to its software.

Explain the difference between R/2, R/3 and S/4HANA.

R/2 was the mainframe generation. R/3 introduced client/server with three tiers (presentation, application, database). S/4HANA is the current suite built on the SAP HANA in-memory database, with a simplified data model and Fiori user interface.

Explain three-tier architecture.

Presentation (user interface), application (business logic on application servers) and database (data storage) run separately, so each can be scaled and maintained independently.

What is the SAP landscape and why do we need it?

Development (configure), Quality (test and train) and Production (live). It prevents untested changes from reaching live data and gives a controlled path for changes using transport requests.

What is a transport request?

A container that carries configuration or development changes from one system to the next through the transport route. Workbench requests carry development objects; Customizing requests carry configuration.

What is a client in SAP?

A self-contained unit within a system with its own master data, user data and customizing (for cross-client settings, the exceptions apply across all clients).

Projects and roles

Greenfield vs Brownfield vs Bluefield?

Greenfield is a new implementation from scratch. Brownfield converts an existing system, keeping history and customizations. Bluefield is a selective approach, moving chosen data, processes or entities.

What are the phases of SAP Activate?

Discover, Prepare, Explore, Realize, Deploy, Run.

What is a roll-out project?

Deploying an existing SAP solution template to a new country, plant or business unit, with local adaptations.

What is the role of a key (super) user?

A business-side expert who supports colleagues, tests changes, validates requirements and acts as the contact point between the business and the project or support team.

Describe how you would handle a support ticket.

Reproduce the issue, check documents and master data, look at the logs or document flow, identify the root cause (data, configuration, authorization or program), fix it in DEV, test in QAS, transport to PRD, and document the resolution and any follow-up.

Data

Master vs transactional data?

Master data is long-lived reference data (materials, vendors, customers). Transactional data records business events using it (orders, receipts, invoices).

What changed for vendor and customer masters in S/4HANA?

They are maintained through the Business Partner concept, which covers both roles in one record.

SAP MM

Walk me through procure-to-pay.

Requirement, purchase requisition, source determination or RFQ, purchase order, goods receipt, invoice verification, payment. The goods receipt updates stock and posts to finance; invoice verification clears the GR/IR account; payment is handled in FI.

What is the GR/IR account?

A clearing account. The goods receipt credits it and the invoice receipt debits it. A balance means goods arrived without an invoice, or an invoice arrived without goods.

Standard price vs moving average price?

Standard price keeps stock at a fixed value, with differences going to a price-difference account. Moving average recalculates the price with each receipt.

What is a material type, and what does it control?

It classifies materials (raw material, finished product, trading goods, service and so on) and controls which views and valuation behaviour apply.

What is automatic account determination?

Configuration that decides which GL accounts are posted for a goods movement, based on the valuation class, movement type and transaction key (for example BSX, WRX, GBB, PRD).

What is a three-way match?

Comparing the purchase order, goods receipt and vendor invoice before payment, with tolerances for quantity and price.

What does MRP do?

It calculates net requirements by comparing demand with stock and expected receipts, then proposes planned orders or purchase requisitions to cover shortages.

What happens if the invoice arrives before the goods receipt?

It depends on the configuration (for example the GR-based invoice verification setting). With goods-receipt-based verification the invoice may be blocked or not allowed until the goods receipt exists.

Name some inventory movement types and what they mean.

101 goods receipt for PO, 102 its reversal, 201 issue to cost centre, 261 issue to production order, 301 plant transfer, 311 storage-location transfer, 561 initial stock, 601 goods issue for delivery.

Other modules and integration

How does MM integrate with FI?

Every valuated goods movement and invoice posts accounting documents automatically, through account determination.

What accounting entries happen at goods issue and billing in SD?

At goods issue: debit cost of goods sold, credit inventory. At billing: debit customer receivable, credit revenue (and tax).

What is the Universal Journal?

In S/4HANA, a single line-item table that stores both financial and controlling data, which removes the need to reconcile FI and CO.

Difference between FI and CO?

FI produces legal external reports; CO supports internal management reporting and cost analysis.

What do BOM and routing describe?

BOM lists components; routing lists operations and work centres used to produce a product.

What is clean core?

Keeping the ERP core free of modifications by extending through supported, upgrade-safe mechanisms such as ABAP Cloud and SAP BTP.

Behavioural prompts to prepare

  • Describe a time you traced a problem back to bad master data.
  • Describe your practice-system project and what you learned from a mistake in it.
  • How do you explain an SAP posting to a non-technical colleague?
  • Why this module and why SAP?

Interview technique: answer using a short structure: define it, explain how it works, give an example from your practice system, and name one limitation or risk. That works for almost any SAP question.
